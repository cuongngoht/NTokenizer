## Claims

1. A GPT model trained only to predict legal Othello moves builds an internal board state that probes can decode. The original paper found nonlinear probes worked better than linear ones, and interventions on the representation changed the model's predicted moves. | "Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task" (Li et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382 | ICLR 2023 | 2022-10 (arXiv) / 2023-05 (ICLR) | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed (ICLR 2023) | high | seminal
2. CORRECTION/REFINEMENT: the Othello board state is linearly encoded once tiles are coded "mine vs. yours" (relative to the player to move) instead of "black vs. white". The model can then be steered with simple vector arithmetic. | "Emergent Linear Representations in World Models of Self-Supervised Sequence Models" (Nanda, Lee, Wattenberg) https://aclanthology.org/2023.blackboxnlp-1.2 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941 | 6th BlackboxNLP Workshop @ EMNLP 2023, pp.16–30 | 2023-09 (arXiv) / 2023-12 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed (workshop) | high | seminal
3. A chess model trained on next-character prediction of real human game strings (PGN) has a linearly decodable board state, with up to ~99.6% probe accuracy. Editing that representation changes its moves, and adding a "player skill" vector raised its win rate by up to 2.6x. | "Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models" (Karvonen) https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15498 | arXiv (venue not confirmed this run; possibly COLM 2024, unverified) | 2024-03 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint (as verified) | medium-high (figures from abstract/summaries) | SOTA-empirical
4. COUNTERWEIGHT: models can pass standard world-model diagnostics, such as next-token validity and probes, while their implicit world models are incoherent under Myhill–Nerode-based metrics (tested on game playing, logic puzzles and NYC navigation). This makes them fragile on related tasks. | "Evaluating the World Model Implicit in a Generative Model" (Vafa et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03689 | NeurIPS 2024 | 2024-06 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | debate
5. Grokking: on small algorithmic datasets such as modular arithmetic, validation accuracy can jump from chance to perfect long after the model has overfit the training set. For modular division this took ~1000x more steps than fitting the training data, and weight decay helps. | "Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets" (Power et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177 | ICLR 2021 workshop paper; arXiv 2022 | 2022-01 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint / workshop (not main-track) | high | seminal
6. Small transformers that grok modular addition learn an algorithm based on discrete Fourier transforms and trigonometric identities, which turns addition into rotation around a circle. This was confirmed by ablations in Fourier space. Training splits into memorization, circuit formation and cleanup phases, and the progress measures (restricted/excluded loss) improve continuously before the grokking jump. | "Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability" (Nanda et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217 | ICLR 2023 | 2023-01 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal
7. Linear probes on Llama-2 activations recover the latitude/longitude of places and the dates of events/entities. The representations hold across scales (world, US, NYC), are robust to prompt variations and unified across entity types, and individual "space/time neurons" exist. The authors' caveat: this is NOT by itself a dynamic causal world model. | "Language Models Represent Space and Time" (Gurnee & Tegmark) https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02207 | ICLR 2024 | 2023-10 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | SOTA-empirical (2023–24)
8. Induction heads, attention heads that complete [A][B]…[A]→[B], form at the same point in training as a sudden jump in in-context learning. Causal evidence comes only from small attention-only models; for large models with MLPs the evidence is correlational. | "In-context Learning and Induction Heads" (Olsson et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895 | Anthropic / Transformer Circuits Thread (arXiv) | 2022-09 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint (not peer-reviewed) | high | seminal
9. Feed-forward (MLP) layers act as key-value memories: keys match textual input patterns, with shallower patterns in lower layers and more semantic ones in upper layers, and values induce distributions over output tokens. Each layer's output combines hundreds of active memories. | "Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories" (Geva et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913 | EMNLP 2021 | 2020-12 (arXiv) / 2021-11 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal
10. Causal tracing locates factual recall in mid-layer MLP modules at the subject's last token. ROME edits a single fact with a rank-one update to one MLP layer, e.g. teaching GPT-J that the Eiffel Tower is in Rome, and the edit generalizes across paraphrases. | "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT" (Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov) https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262 | NeurIPS 2022 | 2022-02 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal
11. Superposition: networks can represent more features than they have dimensions by storing sparse features in superposition. This is one cause of polysemantic neurons, it shows phase changes and geometric (polytope) structure, and toy models can even compute in superposition (e.g. absolute value). | "Toy Models of Superposition" (Elhage et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10652 | Anthropic / Transformer Circuits (arXiv) | 2022-09 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint | high | theory / seminal
12. Sparse autoencoders with up to ~34M features, trained on Claude 3 Sonnet's middle-layer residual stream, yield interpretable features that are often abstract: multilingual, multimodal, and generalizing between concrete and abstract references (e.g. famous people, places, code type signatures, safety-relevant concepts). The authors say the feature set is incomplete and that faithfulness is not rigorously evaluated. This builds on "Towards Monosemanticity" (2023, one-layer transformer). | "Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet" (Templeton et al.) https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/ (not reachable this run; content via search snippets) | Anthropic Transformer Circuits Thread | 2024-05 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint (not peer-reviewed) | medium-high | SOTA-empirical
13. Attribution-graph / circuit-tracing study of Claude 3.5 Haiku. (a) Two-hop reasoning: for the capital of the state containing Dallas, the model activates a "Dallas is in Texas" feature and composes it with "capital of Texas = Austin". Swapping Texas for California changes the output to Sacramento, i.e. the model "combin[es] independent facts … rather than regurgitating a memorized response." (b) Rhyme planning: before writing the line, the model selects an on-topic rhyme word ("rabbit" for "grab it") and writes toward it. Suppressing "rabbit" gives "habit", and injecting "green" gives a line ending in "green". | "Tracing the thoughts of a large language model" (Anthropic, summarizing Lindsey et al. "On the Biology of a Large Language Model" and Ameisen et al. "Circuit Tracing") https://www.anthropic.com/research/tracing-thoughts-language-model | Anthropic research blog / Transformer Circuits Thread | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint (not peer-reviewed) | high (primary org summary read in full) | SOTA-empirical
14. Arithmetic: for 36+59, Claude 3.5 Haiku uses multiple parallel paths, one approximating the magnitude and one computing the last digit exactly, rather than either memorization or the school algorithm. Asked how it did the sum, it describes the standard carry-the-1 method, which means its self-explanation does not match its internal mechanism. | same source as 13 | Anthropic | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint | high | SOTA-empirical
15. Limits stated by Anthropic for claims 13–14: the method "only captures a fraction of the total computation". The observed mechanisms may include artifacts of the tools themselves. Tracing prompts of only tens of words takes a few hours of human effort. | same source as 13 | Anthropic | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint | high | debate (method caveat)
16. 2026 update: concept-injection experiments show that Claude models sometimes detect and correctly name concepts injected into their activations. One summary puts this at ~20% for Opus 4.1. They can also distinguish their own prior outputs from artificial prefills. The authors call this capacity "highly unreliable and context-dependent" and make no claim of human-like self-awareness. This qualifies claim 14: self-reports are only weakly and unreliably tied to internals. | "Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models" (Lindsey) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01828 | arXiv (Anthropic) | 2026-01 (arXiv; an earlier Transformer Circuits version likely appeared in 2025-10, unverified) | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint | medium-high | SOTA-empirical
