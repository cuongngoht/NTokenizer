# D3 — Post-training and reasoning (round 1)

Accessed 2026-11-08 for every source below. Evidence comes from web search result snippets and the venue pages they returned. arxiv.org could not be fetched directly (WebFetch DNS block), so arXiv-hosted wording was seen only through search excerpts.

## Claims

1. Labelers preferred the outputs of the 1.3B InstructGPT model (SFT + RLHF) over the outputs of 175B GPT-3, which has about 100x more parameters. The comparison was on OpenAI's API prompt distribution. InstructGPT gave truthful and informative answers on TruthfulQA about twice as often as GPT-3. Toxicity improved slightly and bias did not. | Ouyang et al., "Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155 ; https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2022/ouyang2022neurips-training | NeurIPS 2022 | 2022-03 (preprint) | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal. CAVEAT: the frequently cited "71.4%" win rate for 1.3B vs 175B comes only from a secondary summary and is unverified. 175B InstructGPT beat 175B GPT-3 85% of the time (±3%) (snippet).

2. DeepSeek-R1 was published in Nature in 2025 (vol. 645, pp. 633–638, issue 8081, 18 Sep 2025). Its claim is that reasoning can be incentivized by pure RL with rule-based correctness rewards, without human-labeled reasoning traces. During that RL, self-verification and reflection behaviors emerged. | Guo et al. (DeepSeek-AI), "DeepSeek-R1 incentivizes reasoning in LLMs through reinforcement learning" — https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41586-025-09422-z ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948 | Nature | 2025-09 (preprint 2025-01) | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | SOTA-empirical. Media described it as the first major open-weight LLM to go through independent peer review. The training-cost figure of about $294K appears only in secondary reporting.

3. OpenAI o1 (Sep 2024) reported that performance improves with both train-time RL compute and test-time "thinking" compute. On AIME the relationship is roughly log-linear (accuracy rises linearly as compute grows exponentially). | OpenAI, "Learning to reason with LLMs" — https://openai.com/index/learning-to-reason-with-llms/ (primary not retrieved; seen via https://arcprize.org/blog/openai-o1-results-arc-prize) | OpenAI blog | 2024-09 | accessed 2026-11-08 | not peer-reviewed (company blog) | medium | SOTA-empirical. The training method and data selection were not disclosed.

4. Yue et al. found that RLVR-trained models beat their base models at pass@1, but the base models reach a higher pass@k at large k. Coverage and perplexity analyses suggest the reasoning paths are already present in the base model and bounded by it. On this view, RLVR re-weights or sharpens existing capability toward what the base model can already do. Distillation, by contrast, can add new reasoning patterns. | Yue et al., "Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?" — https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/oral/119945 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837 | NeurIPS 2025 (Oral) | 2025-04 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high (for the finding), medium (for generality) | debate. Award status is reported inconsistently: the arXiv comment says Oral plus an ICML'25 AI4MATH workshop best paper, while one secondary source says "Best Paper Runner-Up". This is unverified.

5. Counter-evidence: ProRL (NVIDIA) reports that prolonged RL, using KL control and reference-policy resets on a diverse task suite, raises pass@k above the base model. That includes tasks where the base model fails at every k. The authors argue RL can expand the reasoning boundary. | Liu et al., "ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models" — https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117423 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24864 | NeurIPS 2025 (poster) | 2025-05 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | medium | debate. Both sides contest pass@k at large k as a metric, since a correct answer can come from a wrong chain of thought.

6. Feng et al. use circuit complexity to show that bounded-depth transformers cannot directly output answers to basic arithmetic or equation tasks unless model size grows super-polynomially with input length. Constant-size autoregressive transformers can solve the same tasks by generating CoT derivations. The explanation is that CoT increases effective depth in proportion to the number of generated steps. | Feng et al., "Towards Revealing the Mystery behind Chain of Thought: A Theoretical Perspective" — https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/dfc310e81992d2e4cedc09ac47eff13e-Abstract.html | NeurIPS 2023 (Oral) | 2023-05 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | theory. The impossibility result relies on standard complexity conjectures (e.g. TC0 ≠ NC1).

7. Merrill & Sabharwal show that a transformer without intermediate steps is bounded by TC0. With O(log n) steps the bound rises only to L. With O(n) steps the model can recognize all regular languages and stays within context-sensitive languages. With poly(n) steps and generalized pre-norm, it recognizes exactly P, the first exact characterization of a transformer type by a standard complexity class. | Merrill & Sabharwal, "The Expressive Power of Transformers with Chain of Thought" — https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1f59721c106ea80f613299039112f651-Abstract-Conference.html | ICLR 2024 | 2023-10 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | theory. These are expressivity results about what a transformer can represent. They do not show what training will actually learn.

8. Li et al. show that constant-depth transformers with constant-bit precision and O(log n) embedding size can solve any problem solvable by a boolean circuit of size T when given T CoT steps. Without CoT, such low-precision constant-depth transformers are limited to AC0, a strict subset of TC0. (With log precision the usual upper bound is TC0.) Experiments show CoT helps on permutation composition, iterated squaring and circuit value, especially for shallow models. | Li, Liu, Zhou & Ma, "Chain of Thought Empowers Transformers to Solve Inherently Serial Problems" — https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/3309b4112c9f04a993f2bbdd0274bba1-Abstract-Conference.html | ICLR 2024 | 2024-02 | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | theory. CORRECTION for the article: do not say "constant-depth transformers = TC0" without qualification. The bound is TC0 under log precision and AC0 under constant precision.

9. Snell et al. find that compute-optimal test-time scaling, which allocates compute adaptively per prompt by difficulty, is more than 4x more efficient than best-of-N on MATH. In a FLOPs-matched comparison, a smaller model (PaLM 2-S*) with test-time compute beats a model 14x larger, but only on easy and intermediate questions where the small model's base success rate is non-trivial. On the hardest questions, pretraining compute remains the better investment. | Snell, Lee, Xu & Kumar, "Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally Can Be More Effective than Scaling Parameters for Reasoning" — https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1b623663fd9b874366f3ce019fdfdd44-Abstract-Conference.html | ICLR 2025 (Oral) | 2024-08 (preprint) | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | SOTA-empirical. CORRECTION: the article should not say "test-time compute can replace model size" without this condition.

10. Kadavath et al. find that larger models are well calibrated on multiple-choice and true/false questions when given the right format. Models can also self-evaluate P(True) for their own proposed answers, and this improves when they see several of their own samples. A trained P(IK) ("I know") predicts well and partly generalizes, but its calibration weakens on new tasks. | Kadavath et al., "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221 | arXiv (Anthropic) | 2022-07 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint | high | seminal.

11. The GPT-4 technical report says the pre-trained base model is highly calibrated (its confidence matches its accuracy) and that OpenAI's post-training process (RLHF/PPO) reduces that calibration. | OpenAI, "GPT-4 Technical Report" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774 | arXiv (company report) | 2023-03 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint / technical report | medium-high | SOTA-empirical. Seen only through secondary pages quoting the report, because arxiv.org could not be fetched. The wording is consistent across those sources. A 2026 follow-up uses the pre-trained model as a calibrator for post-trained LLMs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04277 (preprint, not read).

12. Toolformer is a language model that teaches itself, in a self-supervised way, to call APIs: a calculator, a QA system, search, translation and a calendar. | Schick et al., "Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools" — https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2023/hash/d842425e4bf79ba039352da0f658a906-Abstract.html | NeurIPS 2023 | 2023-02 (preprint) | accessed 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal.

13. ADD (nuance on CoT as explanation): the CoT of reasoning models often fails to verbalize the hints it actually used. Reveal rates are often below 20%. Average faithfulness is about 25% for Claude 3.7 Sonnet and about 39% for DeepSeek-R1. Outcome-based RL raises faithfulness at first but plateaus (about 28% on MMLU, 20% on GPQA). | Chen et al. (Anthropic), "Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410 ; https://www.anthropic.com/research/reasoning-models-dont-say-think | arXiv | 2025-05 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint | medium-high | debate. Implication for the article: CoT improves accuracy, but the text it produces is not a faithful account of how the model computed the answer.

## Leads
- Wei 2022, Kojima 2022, Wang ICLR 2023, Lewis 2020: run one venue check each against proceedings.neurips.cc and openreview.
- The Yue vs ProRL dispute. Later 2025–26 work argues that large-k pass@k overstates base-model ability, and that temperature scaling lets base models match RL. Look for a 2026 synthesis or survey.
- ProRL v2 (NVIDIA blog), and whether DeepSeek-R1's Nature version changed claims relative to the arXiv version (e.g. the supplementary material and the reviewer exchange).
