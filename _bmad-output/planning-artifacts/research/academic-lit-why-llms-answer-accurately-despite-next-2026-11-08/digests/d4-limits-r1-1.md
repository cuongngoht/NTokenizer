## Claims

1. Pretraining hallucination has a statistical floor: for "arbitrary facts", generative error is lower-bounded via a reduction to binary classification ("Is-It-Valid"), and the hallucination rate is at least about the **singleton rate** (fraction of facts seen exactly once in training, a Good-Turing missing-mass idea). | "Why Language Models Hallucinate" (Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, Zhang), https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664 | OpenAI / arXiv | 2025-09 | accessed 2026-11-08 | preprint (no venue found) | medium-high (snippets of paper + multiple summaries agree) | theory
2. Hallucinations persist after post-training because benchmarks graded 0/1 make guessing always optimal versus abstaining ("I don't know" scores 0). Proposed fix: explicit confidence targets, e.g. +1 correct, 0 IDK, -t/(1-t) for errors, so a model should answer only if confidence > t. | same as #1 | OpenAI / arXiv | 2025-09 | 2026-11-08 | preprint | medium-high | theory / debate
3. Kalai & Vempala: a *calibrated* LM must hallucinate on arbitrary facts at a rate close to the fraction of facts appearing exactly once in training, even with error-free data; no such floor for facts that recur. Explicitly *not* a claim that hallucination is inevitable in general. | "Calibrated Language Models Must Hallucinate", https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14648 (DOI 10.1145/3618260.3649777) | STOC 2024 (ACM) | 2024-06 (arXiv 2023-11) | 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | theory / seminal
4. Reversal curse: models fine-tuned on "A is B" do not generalize to "B is A"; robust across sizes/families, not fixed by data augmentation. GPT-4: ~79% on "Who is Tom Cruise's mother?" vs ~33% on the reverse. | "The Reversal Curse: LLMs trained on 'A is B' fail to learn 'B is A'" (Berglund et al.), https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12288 | ICLR 2024 (per conference header on arXiv PDF; OpenReview page not seen) | 2023-09 / 2024-05 | 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal
5. Mitigation: reverse training (train on original + reversed text, entity-preserving or random-segment reversal) can mitigate and sometimes fully eliminate the reversal curse, without hurting standard tasks at matched data. => the curse is a property of left-to-right training data/objective, not an absolute limit. | "Reverse Training to Nurse the Reversal Curse" (Golovneva, Allen-Zhu, Weston, Sukhbaatar), https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13799 | Meta FAIR / arXiv | 2024-03 | 2026-11-08 | preprint (venue not confirmed) | medium-high | SOTA-empirical
6. Embers of autoregression: LLM accuracy depends on probability of the task, of the target output, and of the input. 5 LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Claude 3, Llama 3, Gemini 1.0), 11 tasks. Example: GPT-4 decodes a shift cipher at 51% when output is a high-probability sentence vs 13% when low-probability. | "Embers of autoregression show how large language models are shaped by the problem they are trained to solve" (McCoy, Yao, Friedman, Hardy, Griffiths), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2322420121 | PNAS (vol/issue not confirmed; DOI prefix is PNAS) | 2024 (arXiv 2023-09) | 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high on findings, medium on exact issue | seminal
7. o1 follow-up: o1-preview substantially outperforms earlier LLMs, especially on rare task variants, but is still probability-sensitive (better accuracy and fewer thinking tokens in high-probability cases). Reasoning optimization "can mitigate but might not fully overcome" probability sensitivity. | "When a language model is optimized for reasoning, does it still show embers of autoregression? An analysis of OpenAI o1", https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01792 | arXiv | 2024-10 | 2026-11-08 | preprint | high | SOTA-empirical
8. Faith and Fate: on multi-digit multiplication, logic grid puzzles and a DP problem (framed as computation graphs), transformers solve compositional tasks by **linearized subgraph matching** (matching seen sub-computations) rather than systematic procedures; accuracy falls quickly with complexity and errors compound. | "Faith and Fate: Limits of Transformers on Compositionality" (Dziri et al.), https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/deb3c28192f979302c157cb653c15e90-Abstract.html | NeurIPS 2023 (spotlight) | 2023-12 | 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal
9. CoT unfaithfulness: biasing features (e.g. reordering few-shot options so the answer is always "(A)") push models to wrong answers, and the CoT rationalizes them without mentioning the bias; accuracy drops up to 36% across 13 BIG-Bench Hard tasks (GPT-3.5, Claude 1.0). | "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting" (Turpin et al.), https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388 ; https://nips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71118 | NeurIPS 2023 | 2023-05 / 2023-12 | 2026-11-08 | peer-reviewed | high | seminal
10. Lanham et al.: interventions on CoT (truncation, inserted mistakes, etc.) show reliance on CoT varies a lot by task; post-hoc reasoning often shows inverse scaling (larger/more capable models less faithful on many tasks). | "Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning", https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702 ; https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning | Anthropic / arXiv | 2023-07 | 2026-11-08 | preprint | medium-high | SOTA-empirical
11. Reasoning models: when they use a hint embedded in the prompt, they verbalize it in CoT only a minority of the time; average faithfulness 25% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) and 39% (DeepSeek R1); lower on misaligned hints (~20% / ~29%). Outcome-based RL improves faithfulness initially then plateaus. | "Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think" (Chen et al., Anthropic), https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410 | Anthropic / arXiv | 2025-04 (blog) / 2025-05 (arXiv) | 2026-11-08 | preprint | high (numbers consistent across sources) | SOTA-empirical
12. GSM-Symbolic: changing only names/numbers in GSM8K templates lowers and spreads out accuracy for all models; GSM-NoOp (adding an irrelevant but plausible clause) causes drops up to ~65%, including for strong models like o1-preview. | "GSM-Symbolic: Understanding the Limitations of Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models" (Mirzadeh et al., Apple), https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229 | arXiv (ICLR 2025 acceptance NOT verified this run) | 2024-10 | 2026-11-08 | preprint as verified | high on findings | debate / SOTA-empirical
13. Counterpoint on GSM-Symbolic: a 2026 re-analysis argues the GSM-Symbolic set shifts toward larger integers vs. GSM-base, so some conclusions about "lack of reasoning" are statistically premature. | "The Importance of Being Statistically Earnest" (title per search snippet), https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28700 | arXiv | 2026-05 | 2026-11-08 | preprint | low-medium (snippet only) | debate
14. "Illusion of the Illusion of Thinking" rebuttal to Apple's "Illusion of Thinking": Tower of Hanoi collapse partly reflects output-token limits (models say they are truncating); River Crossing includes unsolvable instances for N>5 that were scored as failures; asking for a generating function instead of full move lists gives high accuracy (preliminary). v1 listed Claude Opus as co-author, removed in v2. | "The Illusion of the Illusion of Thinking" (A. Lawsen), https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09250 | arXiv comment | 2025-06 | 2026-11-08 | preprint, not peer-reviewed | medium-high | debate
15. Character counting failures are linked to tokenization, but evidence is mixed: CharBench finds performance correlates more with the number of character occurrences than with tokenization properties; "The Strawberry Problem" argues tokenization is the fundamental limit. | Fu et al. "Why Do LLMs Struggle to Count Letters" https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18626 (2024-12); CharBench https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02591 (2025-08); https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14172 (2025-05) | arXiv | 2024-12 to 2025-08 | 2026-11-08 | preprints | medium | debate

## Corrections / nuances for the article
- Kalai et al. 2025 is a preprint (OpenAI), not peer-reviewed as far as found. The bound covers *arbitrary* (pattern-less) facts; it does not say all hallucination is unavoidable. The 2024 STOC paper is the peer-reviewed theory anchor.
- Reversal curse is real but not fundamental: reverse training mitigates it.
- Say "o1 reduces but does not remove" embers of autoregression.
- Reasoning-trace text is not a reliable account of the model's actual computation (Turpin, Lanham, Chen et al.).
- Apple papers are contested; present them as one side of a live debate, citing the Lawsen rebuttal (itself non-peer-reviewed, partly preliminary) and the 2026 GSM-Symbolic re-analysis.
- Do not state "tokenization is THE reason LLMs miscount letters" flatly; say it is one contributing factor, with mixed evidence.
