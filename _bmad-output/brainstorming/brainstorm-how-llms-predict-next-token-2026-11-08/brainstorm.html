Layer 1 · Query projection

Question Storming

Twenty-five questions with no answers allowed. Each one is a query vector looking back over the context: what is the model attending to, and why?

25 ideas → residual += Δ

  1. q001

    What exactly is a single logit measuring: plausibility, frequency, or the model's belief about the world?

  2. q002

    At which layer does the correct next token first become the argmax under a logit lens, and is that layer stable across prompts?

  3. q003

    Does a Vietnamese model "decide" a multi-syllable word at its first BPE piece, or re-decide at each piece?

  4. q004

    How much of next-token accuracy comes from the last 32 tokens of context versus everything before them?

  5. q005

    When the distribution is sharp (p>0.9), which components (attention vs MLP) contributed the most to the winning logit?

  6. q006

    Is there a measurable "uncertainty direction" in the residual stream that predicts entropy before the unembedding?

  7. q007

    Do tone marks (dấu) in Vietnamese get predicted by different heads than base syllables?

  8. q008

    How many training tokens does a fact need to appear before the model reliably completes it (singleton rate in practice)?

  9. q009

    Does RoPE make the model attend by relative distance in a way that creates "copy the token N back" circuits earlier in training?

  10. q010

    Does GQA (shared K/V heads) reduce the number of distinct induction heads that can form?

  11. q011

    Which is learned first on viwiki: syllable boundaries, word boundaries, or sentence punctuation?

  12. q012

    Can we predict which prompts will produce hallucinations from the entropy of the first answer token?

  13. q013

    Is temperature 0.7 + top-p 0.9 picking a different "mode" of the distribution or just trimming noise?

  14. q014

    Does repetition penalty damage factual recall when the correct answer repeats a prompt token?

  15. q015

    How does loss on numbers/dates compare with loss on common words, and does it ever converge?

  16. q016

    What fraction of the 32k vocab is ever predicted with p>0.5 in any validation context?

  17. q017

    Do weight-tied embeddings (if used) force input and output meaning to share geometry, and does that help prediction?

  18. q018

    When the model is wrong with high confidence, what does the context have in common?

  19. q019

    Can the model's own loss on a sentence tell us whether the sentence is in the training set (memorization signal)?

  20. q020

    Is next-token prediction on Vietnamese harder than on English per character, per syllable, or per token?

  21. q021

    Do attention patterns collapse to "attend to BOS" when the model has nothing useful to copy (attention sinks)?

  22. q022

    Does the model plan ahead (e.g. choose a verb before its subject finishes) at our tiny scale, or is that only a large-model phenomenon?

  23. q023

    How much does the val loss improve for each doubling of block_size, and where does it plateau?

  24. q024

    Does the KV-cache eviction strategy in generate() change predictions compared with full recomputation?

  25. q025

    What is the smallest model in this repo that can do 2-digit addition after fine-tuning, with and without scratchpad tokens?

Layer 3 · Sign flip

Assumption Reversal

Twenty common beliefs, each pushed down to a low logit while the reversal takes the probability mass.

20 ideas → residual += Δ

  1. r051
    prediction means guessing

    prediction at low loss is the hardest form of understanding; open the article with a compression-game demo

  2. r052
    the model outputs words

    it outputs a full probability distribution; the word is our sampling choice

  3. r053
    randomness makes it unreliable

    randomness lives only in sampling; show T=0 determinism

  4. r054
    it thinks one token at a time

    internal planning (rhyme example) means it looks ahead

  5. r055
    it retrieves facts like a database

    facts are reconstructed from distributed features, which is why rare facts break

  6. r056
    more data = memorizing more

    more data forces compression into rules (grokking story)

  7. r057
    the model knows how it reasons

    the 36+59 case shows its explanation is a story, not a trace

  8. r058
    chain-of-thought shows its reasoning

    CoT is a scratchpad that helps but is only 25-39% faithful

  9. r059
    hallucination is a bug

    it is the expected output of a calibrated predictor on singleton facts

  10. r060
    bigger is always better

    a 1.3B instruction-tuned model beat 175B GPT-3 for users

  11. r061
    prediction cannot be creative

    sampling from a sharp-but-wide distribution is controlled creativity

  12. r062
    tokens are words

    show how a Vietnamese sentence splits into BPE pieces and why that matters

  13. r063
    accuracy comes from pretraining

    for modern assistants, RL with verifiable rewards does much of the work

  14. r064
    the model sees the whole sentence

    causal mask means it never sees the future; show the mask matrix

  15. r065
    emergence is magic

    it is often a metric artifact, but sometimes a real loss threshold

  16. r066
    a probe finding a world model proves it exists

    Vafa et al. show probes can be fooled

  17. r067
    errors are random

    errors track training-data probability (embers of autoregression)

  18. r068
    next-token loss is a weak signal

    every token is a supervised example, trillions of labels for free

  19. r069
    the answer is chosen at the end

    logit lens shows the answer often forms mid-network

  20. r070
    English and Vietnamese work the same

    tonal syllables and tokenizer splits change difficulty

What the stream decided

After six layers of ideas, the final residual is read out. The insights are what survived the unembedding, and four directions win the probability mass.

  1. insight 1Three ideas are one

    logit lens + top-5 printer + entropy heatmap all expose the distribution, so build one 'prediction microscope' tool that powers both experiments and the explainer visuals

  2. insight 2The grokking mini-lab is the keystone

    cheapest experiment (CPU minutes), most vivid evidence that prediction learns algorithms, and it doubles as the explainer's climax chapter

  3. insight 3The thread

    Frequency is the thread connecting accuracy and failure: synthetic-world fact frequency study + calibration plot + reversal test together reproduce singleton-rate hallucination and embers of autoregression on our own model

  4. insight 4Vietnamese is the differentiator

    tokenizer splits and tone marks turn generic LLM claims into original findings nobody else has on viwiki

  5. insight 5Rule from the Critic

    pair every probe with an intervention (patching/ablation) and phrase results as 'represents/computes at small scale'

Chosen directions

  1. microscope–

    (1) prediction microscope tooling

  2. grokking–

    (2) grokking lab

  3. frequency–

    (3) frequency-vs-accuracy study

  4. explainer–

    (4) English explainer docs/how_llms_predict.md built around those three with the analogies (zip compression, weather forecast, detective novel, GPS rerouting)

Bar lengths are illustrative logits, not measured values. Slide the temperature: at low T the first direction takes nearly all the mass, while at high T they all pull level. They are four parts of one plan, so you want the whole distribution, not only the argmax.
